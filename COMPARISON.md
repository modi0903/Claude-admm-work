## Read this before the table

**Fairness decisions taken here, so a reviewer does not have to guess.** TASER
is splitting, not ADMM (daggered, kept because it shares group, part and
discipline with ADMIN). Peccin's area cell is empty because Intel LEs are not
Slice LUTs. AccelMPC's power is absent because the paper states none. Escarate
is cited but not tabulated. Our own row is the smallest problem in the table.

**This is not a ranking, and the paper claims no efficiency win over any row.**
Each work solves a different problem, at a different size, on a different
device, to an accuracy that is usually unstated. We deliberately compute no
normalised metric (LUT·s, energy per variable, solves/s/LUT): with accuracy
targets unstated, any such number would be arithmetic dressed as evidence.

Our row is the smallest problem on the smallest part — N=8 on a $100 Artix-7
35T, against ZCU106, Alveo U50 and Virtex-7 690T parts. Absolute LUT counts
across those fabrics compare fabrics, not designs.

## What the table shows — three points, none of them speed

1. **Wordlength is assumed, not derived.** Every fixed-point row states a
   single wordlength as a given: 18 b, 14 b, 24 b, 32 b. None says where it
   came from. Jerez et al. are the exception in kind — they give a bound, not
   a chosen number, and their empirical table stops at 18 fraction bits. Ours
   is 11 bits, derived and then confirmed bit-exact on routed silicon and on
   a board.

2. **Power is usually estimated, when it is reported at all.** Two rows report
   none; three report vendor or post-implementation estimates; one measures a
   datacentre card; one gives a figure with no method. Ours is post-route with
   switching activity from gate-level simulation of the regression vectors,
   Vivado Confidence High. The vectorless number for the same build is ~10x
   too high — that is the size of the gap between these methodologies.

3. **Nobody reports the full set.** No prior ADMM accelerator found here
   reports resources, activity-based power, a *met* Fmax, and a per-solve time
   together for a fixed-point design. This is the honest framing of our
   measurement contribution.

## Row-by-row fairness notes

- **Jerez 2014** — the primary prior art for the error model, but not a
  resource datapoint: it reports multiplier counts (216 at P=1) and projected
  sample rates, no LUT/FF/BRAM, no ADMM power. The 400 MHz is assumed in the
  paper, not a met constraint. Cite for the analysis, not the table.
- **ADMIN 2021** — closest workload (MIMO detection, ADMM, fixed point) and
  the fairest *architecture* comparison, but 16x16 complex at 64-QAM is a much
  bigger problem than N=8 real, and no FPGA power is published (the power
  numbers in that paper are 28 nm ASIC).
- **TASER 2016** — forward-backward splitting, not ADMM. Included because it
  is the same group, same part, same fixed-point discipline, and its 0.6 W is
  a tool estimate, which is the point being made.
- **Wu 2022** — their headline (67% LUT, 60% power) is against an **FP32**
  baseline; ours is Q2.16 fixed narrowed to Q2.9, so the float-to-fixed win is
  already banked in our baseline. Their ADMM fixed-point rows also corroborate
  our negative result: uniform FXP24 6775 LUT vs mixed FXP20->24 6807 LUT.
- **Hamadouche 2023** — closest in spirit (fixed-point ADMM, LASSO, dynamic
  power reported) and the only row whose dynamic-power figure is the same kind
  of quantity as ours, though post-implementation rather than SAIF-annotated.
  Their problem size for the HLS tables is not stated, so per-solve cycles are
  not comparable.
- **RSQP 2023** and **Zhang 2025** — datacentre and embedded-SoC accelerators
  two to three orders of magnitude larger in variables and watts. They are in
  the table to show the range of what "ADMM accelerator" means, not to be
  beaten.
- **AccelMPC 2026** — VERIFIED from the PDF (2026-09-20). Artix-7 100T at
  100 MHz, 32-bit fixed point, TinyMPC-style ADMM, 3.33 us per
  (iteration x horizon step). Closest fabric family to ours. It reports
  utilisation only as percentages (Fig. 6, H=40, k=10: compute 24.1% staged /
  55.8% full sparse, BRAM 26.7% / 58.1%) and no absolute power; "FPGA power is
  obtained from post-route power analysis combined with the measured solve
  time". Dividing the figure's energy labels by its latency labels implies
  ~0.68 W (staged) and ~0.33 W (full sparse), but that is OUR arithmetic on
  their figure, not a number they state — it stays in the data file's note and
  out of the table.
- **Peccin 2020** — VERIFIED from the PDF. Altera MAX10 (DE10-Lite) at 50 MHz,
  fixed point 16 b with 10 fractional bits, 30 662 logic elements (62%), 7 691
  registers, 49.125 KB memory (24%), 40 embedded multipliers (14%), 0.36 us per
  iteration. Logic elements are not Slice LUTs, so the LUT cell is left empty
  rather than converted. This is the most useful row for our argument: a
  10-fractional-bit choice stated with no derivation, on a low-cost board.
- **Escarate 2022 — excluded from the table, cited in the text.** ZCU104 via
  Vitis HLS; reports only execution time (FPGA 0.365 / 0.621 / 1.567 ms mean at
  1 / 15 / 50 iterations vs CPU 0.550 / 8.240 / 27.469 ms), no resources, no
  power, no stated number format, and the authors call their results "un piso
  referencial" obtained with default HLS settings. A row would be all n/r.
